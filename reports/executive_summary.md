## 2. Answers to Core Business Questions

### Question 1: How does revenue change over time? Are there seasonal patterns?
- **Finding:** E-commerce sales exhibit pronounced Q4 seasonality. In 2010, monthly revenue surged from **£591,894.66 in August** to a peak of **£1,437,390.37 in November (+142.8%)**, driven by holiday wholesale purchasing. The same pattern repeated in 2011, peaking in November at **£1,461,756.24**.
- **Quarterly Slump:** January and February consistently experience sharp pullbacks (e.g., January 2011 dropped by -56.3% following the holiday peak).
- **Business Implication:** Inventory procurement and warehouse staffing must scale dynamically starting in late August to support peak Q4 fulfilment.

### Question 2: Which products generate the most revenue?
- **Finding:** The top-grossing merchandise items are giftware and decorative accessories:
  1. **REGENCY CAKESTAND 3 TIER** (`22423`): **£344,563.25** across 27,577 units (3,918 orders)
  2. **WHITE HANGING HEART T-LIGHT HOLDER** (`85123A`): **£263,109.67** across 96,147 units (5,365 orders)
  3. **RED RETROSPOT JUMBO BAG** (`85099B`): **£183,454.83** across 98,349 units (3,989 orders)
- **Concentration:** Top 10 items account for over £1.8M in revenue. Postage fees (`POST`, `DOT`) and manual accounting items (`M`) also represent significant turnover (£322k - £340k each).

### Question 3: Which international markets contribute most outside the UK?
- **Finding:** While the **United Kingdom accounts for 85.9% of total revenue**, international expansion is heavily led by European wholesale hubs:
  1. **EIRE (Ireland):** **£615,519.58** (33.0% of non-UK sales)
  2. **Netherlands:** **£548,524.47** (29.4% of non-UK sales)
  3. **Germany:** **£417,988.62** (22.4% of non-UK sales)
  4. **France:** **£328,172.10** (17.6% of non-UK sales)
  5. **Australia:** **£167,072.81** (8.9% of non-UK sales)
- **Insight:** EIRE and Netherlands exhibit substantially higher average spend per customer than UK retail accounts, consistent with regional distributor and wholesaler ordering profiles.

### Question 4: How much revenue comes from repeat customers vs. one-time buyers?
- **Finding:** **72.39% of customers (4,255 buyers)** are repeat purchasers, and they drive **96.80% of customer-attributed revenue (£17,175,255.35)**.
- **Spend Asymmetry:** Average lifetime spend for repeat buyers is **£4,036.49**, compared to just **£350.08** for one-time buyers (an 11.5x multiplier).
- **Strategic Priority:** Customer retention and account management are overwhelmingly the primary drivers of enterprise value. Acquisition without retention yields negligible returns.

### Question 5: Which customers have the highest observed purchase value?
- **Finding:** The top 10 customers represent **£3,354,846.54** (nearly 19% of customer revenue).
  - Top customer (`18102`): **£598,215.18** across 145 orders.
  - Second customer (`14646`, Netherlands): **£523,346.93** across 151 orders.
  - Third customer (`14156`, EIRE): **£313,366.07** across 149 orders.
- **Risk Assessment:** High revenue concentration among a small cohort of high-volume wholesale accounts creates supplier vulnerability. Dedicated key account managers (KAMs) are strongly recommended.

### Question 6: How do cancellations affect gross sales?
- **Finding:** Total cancellations reached **£1,527,415.00** across 19,494 transactions (8,292 cancelled invoices).
- **Rate Stability:** Cancellation loss as a percentage of gross sales remained steady between **6.0% and 9.5%** month-over-month. In November 2011, cancellations totalled £108,187.72 against £1,569,943.96 gross sales (6.89%).
- **Operational Takeaway:** Cancellations track order volume rather than quality spikes, confirming standard wholesale re-ordering adjustments rather than catastrophic product defect surges.

### Strategic Hypotheses to Test:
1. **At Risk Account Win-Back:** The 824 "At Risk" accounts historically spent £1.63M (avg ~5 orders). A targeted B2B re-engagement campaign offering volume rebate tiers could test whether dormant wholesale accounts can be reactivated.
2. **Early Repurchase Trigger:** 1,523 "Hibernating" customers made only 1.25 orders before vanishing. Testing automated replenishment check-ins at Day 45 post-first purchase could lift the first-to-second order conversion rate.

## 4. Analytical Assumptions & Limitations
- **Missing Customer IDs:** 236,870 rows (22.3% of cleaned transactions) lacked a Customer ID (typical of guest checkout or anonymous sales). These transactions were included in product and macro revenue totals but excluded from RFM and customer-level metrics.
- **Absence of COGS:** Cost of Goods Sold is unavailable; figures reflect gross and net revenue, not profit.
- **Wholesale Skew:** Large order quantities and repeat frequencies reflect B2B distributor behaviour rather than purely consumer retail.
